黄历 Huangli: the Chinese almanac for modern activities

Add your own activities and the almanac tells you which days favor them and which to avoid. Not only weddings and moving house, but a software launch, a first date, a job interview or a product release: plans you will not find in any other Chinese almanac. Each answer comes with its reason and the next good days.

Huangli is a free app for the traditional Chinese almanac (黄历 Huangli, 老黄历 Lao Huangli, 通胜 Tung Shing, 通书 Tong Shu) and the Chinese lunar calendar (农历 Nongli, also called 阴历 Yinli; 万年历, the perpetual calendar). It tells you which activities are suitable or to be avoided on any day, and, unlike other almanacs, it shows why: the exact rule, the chapter of the source text, and the original quotation.

What makes it different

  • Traceable to a primary source. The daily Suitable / Avoid lists (宜忌) are computed from the imperial Treatise on Harmonizing Times and Distinguishing Directions (欽定協紀辨方書, 1739): its spirit rules, its activity lists, its six-grade table for weighing good spirits against bad ones (宜忌等第表) and its linkage rules (鋪註條例).
  • Checked against the book itself. Days from the Treatise's own printed monthly tables (月表) were transcribed from scans and are used as tests; the app's results match them. The lunar calendar is cross-checked from 1900 to 2100.
  • Follows the Treatise's corrections. Where the Treatise quotes an older list and corrects it in a note (按), the correction is used. The popular almanac (Tong Shu) reading is available as an option, so you can see why almanacs disagree.
  • Shows disagreement openly. When some spirits favour an activity and others forbid it, it appears under "Mixed" with the Treatise's verdict, because almanacs that skip the grade table often print the opposite.
  • Made for modern life. Plans such as a first date, a job interview, a software release or a product launch are matched to the classical activities, with the reasoning shown.
  • Made for readers new to it. Plain-English summaries, clashing birth years listed by year, and an introduction to yin and yang, the five elements, the ten stems, twelve branches and the sixty-day cycle.

Questions

What is the Chinese almanac (Huangli)?

The Huangli (黄历, also Lao Huangli, Tong Shu or Tung Shing) is the traditional Chinese almanac. For every day of the Chinese lunar calendar (农历 Nongli, 阴历 Yinli) it lists the activities that are suitable (宜) and those to avoid (忌), together with the day's stems and branches, clashing zodiac animal, lucky hours and spirits.

What do 宜 (yi) and 忌 (ji) mean?

宜 means suitable: activities favoured on that day. 忌 means avoid. Huangli computes both from the rules of the 1739 imperial Treatise and shows the rule and source for each item.

Is today a good day to move house, get married or sign a contract?

Add the activity under My activities. The app rates today for it, explains why, and lists the next suitable days.

Why do different almanacs give different answers?

Popular almanacs copy lists from each other, and some old lists contain errors that the Treatise itself corrected. Many also forbid an activity whenever any bad spirit is present, while the Treatise weighs good and bad spirits with a six-grade table. Huangli follows the Treatise and shows where spirits disagree.

How to read it

"Suitable" and "Avoid" are the Treatise's verdicts for its 60 classical activities. The mappings from modern activities are the app's own interpretations and are labelled as such. "Lucky day" refers only to the yellow-path count and does not override the day's spirits. The almanac is a cultural tradition, not a guarantee of outcomes.
